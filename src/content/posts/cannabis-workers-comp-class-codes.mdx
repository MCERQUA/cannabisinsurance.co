Workers' compensation is not optional for cannabis businesses with employees — in most states, it is a legal requirement regardless of industry. But beyond the legal mandate, getting workers' comp right for a cannabis business means understanding how class codes work and why the wrong classification creates real problems. Here is what cannabis operators need to know.

## Why Class Codes Matter

Workers' compensation class codes are numerical codes assigned by rating organizations like the National Council on Compensation Insurance (NCCI) or by individual states. Each code corresponds to a category of work — a description of what the employee actually does — and carries a specific rate per $100 of payroll.

When an insurer assigns class codes to your workers, they are pricing the risk of injury for each type of work performed. A dispensary budtender has a different injury risk profile than a cannabis trimmer doing repetitive manual labor in a cultivation facility. A delivery driver has a different exposure than a processing technician working with extraction equipment.

Getting class codes right matters for three reasons:

1. **Premium accuracy:** Wrong codes mean you are either overpaying for coverage or underpaying in ways that will be corrected at audit — often with a surprise additional premium.
2. **Coverage integrity:** Workers' comp provides benefits to injured employees. If an employee was misclassified and their work category is not covered by the policy as written, their claim may face complications.
3. **Audit exposure:** Every workers' comp policy is subject to annual audit. If your actual payroll breakdown by class code differs from what was estimated at policy inception, you receive an audit bill — or credit — at the end of the policy year.

## Class Codes for Cannabis Dispensaries

Dispensary operations typically involve several distinct work categories:

**Retail salespersons (Budtenders):** The primary public-facing workers in a cannabis dispensary are classified similarly to retail store workers. The work involves customer interaction, cash handling, product handling, and general retail operations. Injury risks include slip-and-fall injuries, robbery-related injuries, and repetitive handling of products.

**Managers and supervisors:** Dispensary management typically carries a clerical or supervisory class code, which carries a lower rate than frontline retail workers.

**Security personnel:** If your dispensary employs security guards, they are classified under a guard or watchperson code, which carries a higher rate reflecting the nature of security work.

**Delivery drivers:** Cannabis delivery drivers are classified under a delivery or driver code appropriate to the weight class and type of vehicle operated. Commercial vehicle operators carry higher workers' comp rates than retail workers.

## Class Codes for Cannabis Cultivators

Cultivation operations have a different risk profile than dispensaries — physical, repetitive labor in an agricultural-adjacent environment.

**Trimmers and plant handlers:** The repetitive manual work of trimming cannabis — hours of fine motor work with trimming scissors or machines — creates significant musculoskeletal exposure. Trimmers are classified under codes that reflect light manufacturing or agricultural labor, depending on the state.

**Irrigation and feeding workers:** Employees managing irrigation systems, nutrient mixing, and plant feeding tasks are classified based on their specific work tasks. Handling nutrient concentrates and pesticides adds chemical exposure to the risk profile.

**Equipment operators:** Workers operating heavy equipment, forklifts, or other machinery in cultivation facilities carry class codes reflecting equipment operator risks.

**Pest and disease management workers:** Employees who apply pesticides, fungicides, or other treatments in cultivation facilities face chemical exposure risks that must be properly classified.

## Class Codes for Cannabis Processors and Extractors

Processing and extraction operations present specialized risks that require careful classification.

**Extraction technicians:** Workers operating butane, CO2, ethanol, or other extraction equipment face specific risks — chemical exposure, pressure vessel hazards, and flammability risks depending on the extraction method. These workers are classified under chemical processing or manufacturing codes with rates reflecting those hazards.

**Edible manufacturing workers:** Employees producing cannabis edibles in a kitchen or manufacturing environment are classified under food manufacturing codes appropriate to the size and type of operation.

**Packaging and labeling workers:** Employees doing repetitive packaging and labeling work are typically classified under light manufacturing or production worker codes.

## The Audit Process

At the end of every workers' comp policy year, the insurer audits your actual payroll to reconcile with the estimated payroll used to calculate your initial premium. This is standard — every workers' comp policy is audited.

If your actual payroll was higher than estimated, you owe additional premium. If it was lower, you receive a credit or refund. If your class codes were incorrect — if workers were assigned to the wrong codes — the audit corrects this, often resulting in significant premium adjustments.

The audit process is where misclassification becomes costly. A cultivation operation that incorrectly classified all workers as clerical employees will face a significant audit bill when the insurer discovers the actual work being performed.
